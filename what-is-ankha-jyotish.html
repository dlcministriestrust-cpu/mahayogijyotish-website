What is Ankha Jyotish?

Mahayogi Ankha Jyotish unites numerology and Jyotish. It is a number-based form of Prashna: a Vedic chart cast for a question, drawn from a number the person asking chooses.

Jyotish and Prashna

Jyotish, the "science of light," is the astrology of India's Vedic tradition. Most people know astrology through the birth chart, drawn from the time, date and place of birth. A lesser-known branch is Prashna, which casts a chart for the moment a question is asked. Prashna needs no birth chart. The moment of the question takes the place of the birth data, and the chart shows where the matter stands and how it is likely to unfold.

Why a number?

Anka is the Sanskrit word for number, and Ankha, the name of this system, comes from it. In number-based Prashna, the number you choose takes the place of the clock as the doorway into the chart. The range of 1 to 108 is not arbitrary: the zodiac's 12 signs, each divided into 9 parts, make 108 divisions, and 108 is held sacred across Indian tradition.

The principle is simple: a sincere question and the number that arises with it belong to the same moment, so the chart drawn from that number mirrors the question.

How the chart is read

The chart is read by the classical rules of Parashari Jyotish: which planets stand for the person asking and for the matter, how strong and well placed they are, how they aspect and support one another, and how they ripen in the deeper divisional chart. Mahayogi Ankha Jyotish adds its own casting formula and a few additional rules for arrangements traditional Jyotish does not have, such as one planet occupying more than one house at the same time. This is possible because the chart places each planet by its energetic influence in a sign and house, not by its physical position in the sky.

How to ask well

  • Ask one clear question about something that matters to you now.
  • Hold the question lightly in mind and choose whatever number comes to you.
  • If the answer is unclear, refine the question. You may have combined several questions into one, or asked something vague. Rephrase it and ask again.
  • If you don't like an answer, don't ask again right away. You will likely get a different chart, but generally the same answer when it's all added up. It's best to wait a while before asking again, unless something has changed.
  • Avoid testing the system. The intent behind testing is not the same as the intent behind a sincere question, and it could give erroneous results. The clearest and most accurate answers come when the question and the intent behind them are aligned.
  • Ask about real situations as they stand now; the system works best when the question is grounded in the present reality.
