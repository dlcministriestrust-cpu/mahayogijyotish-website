What is Ankha Jyotish?

Mahayogi Ankha Jyotish unites numerology and Jyotish in a number-based form of Prashna: a Vedic chart for a question, drawn from a number the questioner chooses.

Jyotish and Prashna

Jyotish, the "science of light," is the astrology of India's Vedic tradition. Most people know astrology as a birth chart, created from one's time, date and place of birth. A lesser-known form is Prashna, the art of creating a chart from the moment a question is asked. Prashna needs no natal chart to give answers. The moment the question is asked provides the birth data needed to erect a chart that shows how the matter stands and how things are likely to unfold.

Why a number?

Anka is the Sanskrit word for number, and Ankha, the name of this system, comes from it. In number-based Prashna, the questioner's chosen number takes the place of the clock as the doorway into the chart. The range 1 to 108 is not arbitrary: the zodiac's 12 signs, each divided into 9 parts, make 108 divisions, and 108 is held sacred across Indian tradition.

The principle is simple. A sincere question and the number that arises with it belong to the same moment. The chart grown from that number is a mirror of the question.

How the chart is read

Once cast, the chart is read by the classical rules of Parashari Jyotish: which planets stand for the questioner and for the matter, their dignity and strength, how they aspect and support one another, and how they ripen in the deeper divisional chart. Mahayogi Ankha Jyotish adds its own casting formula and a slightly modified rule set. These cover arrangements traditional Jyotish does not show, such as the same planet occupying more than one house at once. This is possible because the chart places each planet by its energetic influence in a sign and house, not by its physical position in the sky.

How to ask well

  • Ask one clear question about something that matters to you now
  • Choose whatever number comes to you while holding the question lightly in your mind
  • If the answer is unclear, refine the question. You may have lumped several questions together, or asked something vague or ambiguous. Rephrase and ask again
  • If you don't like an answer, don't ask again right away. An immediate repeat comes from testing or dissatisfaction. Trying to force an answer you like has a different intent behind it than the original question, and so does testing. You will probably still get the same answer, but the best answers come when the question and the intent are in alignment. Wait a while; if the situation has moved, asking again later will show it
  • Avoid hypothetical situations. Ask about real situations that exist now; the system works best when questions are grounded in current reality
